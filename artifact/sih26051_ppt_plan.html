08 2-minute presentation script

The problem (25s)

DRDO's brief is about shelters for extreme, high-altitude, remote regions like Ladakh. In practice, these shelters get built to a fixed, generalized design — and that design isn't checked against the specific site's climate. The result: a shelter that's warm under daytime sun but drops close to outdoor temperature after sunset, because nobody modelled its actual heat loss before building it.

The solution (25s)

We've built a software platform that closes that gap. A designer picks a location and a shelter design — geometry, materials, orientation — and the platform pulls live weather and long-term solar climatology for that exact site, then runs an hourly, physics-based thermal simulation to predict indoor temperature, solar gain, and heat loss before a single wall is built.

How it works (30s)

Underneath, it's a two-node resistance-capacitance heat-balance model — standard building physics, not a black box — fed by a material-property library and live climate data. A multi-criteria optimizer then automatically generates and ranks design candidates on comfort, heat retention, solar utilization and cost, with sensitivity analysis showing exactly which change — orientation, insulation, window area — moves the comfort score the most.

What we've built (25s)

This isn't a concept slide — it's a working prototype today. It fetches live weather for any Indian location, runs the full simulation, and produces a scored, ranked, explainable recommendation, with every computed number traceable back to its formula.

Why it matters (15s)

The same model already covers more than defence shelters — disaster relief, seed and produce storage, livestock shelter — one platform, evaluated against real climate, for anywhere it's needed.

09 Judge perspective

Strongest parts

  • A real, transparent physics model (documented, reproducible formulas) — not a claimed black-box "AI will handle it."
  • A working end-to-end prototype today, not a plan — with live weather, not hardcoded demo data.
  • Optimization + sensitivity analysis is genuine decision support, not a single fixed recommendation.
  • Scope beyond one building type — defence, disaster relief, agricultural storage — from the same engine.

Where judges may push back

  • No field validation yet against a real, instrumented shelter — the model is unproven against measured reality.
  • Single-zone, transient approximation, not a full CFD — a judge may ask why not a validated commercial tool.
  • Team/Product identity isn't finalized (Team ID, Team Name, product name all placeholders) — decide these before the actual submission.

What to emphasize in Q&A: the "Explain Calculation" transparency is itself the answer to "why not ANSYS/a validated tool" — every number is auditable rather than trusted blind, and the validation module is already built and waiting on field data, not an afterthought.

What's missing and should be decided before submission: Team ID, Team Name, and the product name (see § Product names) — all three are placeholders in every source file today.
